Read 'moved%' RELATIVE TO ITS OWN same-min/noise columns, not against an assumed 50%:
when A and B's minutes are far apart (up to 30, the maximum on a 60-minute circle),
baseline sits near the worst possible starting point, so almost ANY perturbation --
including pure noise -- has good odds of looking 'closer' by chance. The noise and
same-minute columns above already reflect that same geometry, so if real_b's numbers
stay close to noise's at the same layer/position (not clearly higher), and 'chg%' stays
low across the WHOLE sweep including the vision-encoder ceiling, THAT null result is
the finding: the angle information is present but the model's answer doesn't use it.

CAUTION -- these 'moved toward TRUE minute' numbers are further confounded on top of the
chance-level geometry above: the model states the TRUE minute correctly only ~2-3% of
the time even when looking straight at the source, so 'moved%'/'chg%' here are capped by
that baseline failure rate regardless of what the patch actually did. See the
TRANSFER-TO-BASELINE summary below for the metric that isn't confounded this way --
compare it, not this table, when judging whether the intervention had a real effect.
